Learn Marketing Analytics with Phoebe · Partnership session 3 of 4

Intake and the service menu

"Can you do market research?" is the ask a data team serving marketing hears most and answers worst. It arrives with no decision, no owner, no date and no edge, so it either gets refused, which leaves somebody deciding on anecdote, or swallowed whole, which starves the work only data can do. A front door fixes both. Tonight is the intake template that turns any ask into a business question, a decision, a decision owner, a deadline and what changes on the answer; the one-page menu that tells marketing, brand and BD what data offers and how long each thing takes; and a list, on the same page, of what is not on the menu and who owns it instead.

Part 1 · covers why a front door

A front door makes every ask smaller

4 min live

Most asks are bigger when they arrive than when they are understood. "Can you do market research on Japan?" sounds like forty hours. Asked what decision it serves, who decides and by when, it becomes "the BD lead needs to know what our own orders already say about Japan before she briefs an agency next week", which is three. The front door is where that shrinking happens, every time, without depending on the analyst's mood that day.

LiveWhat the front door is, and what it is not3 min▶

It is one short form, the same for every requester, that the data team fills in with the requester in a ten-minute conversation, or that the requester fills in alone for small asks. It is where the knowledge tag from Partnership session 2 gets decided, and where a data C gets its deliverable and date.

It is not a bureaucratic gate. If the form takes longer than the work, the form is wrong. Small standard asks from the menu (an email send read, a before and after on a PR hit) should go straight through on a one-line request. The door is for asks that are vague, big, or not obviously data's.

Running the conversation itself, the discovery questions and how to listen for the real need, is taught in Asking the Right Questions as a DA ↗. This page is the form that conversation fills.

Self-studyWhy the requester should own the form, not the analyst2 min▶

A form the analyst fills in after the meeting is the analyst's interpretation. A form the requester signs is an agreement. The difference shows up three weeks later, when the requester remembers asking for something bigger: a form in their words, with their decision and their date, settles it without anybody having to be right.

Real world

The inbox that became a queue. A data team that took asks over chat, email and hallway conversations could never say how many asks it had, let alone which were late. One shared form, with five required fields, turned the same volume into a queue anyone could read, and the first month's surprise was that a third of the asks were the same four standard reads. Those went onto the menu. Constructed example; the finding is a common one.

Part 2 · covers the intake template

The intake template

6 min live

Five fields the requester owns, then three the data team adds. The first five are the four questions from Angles session 1 with the business question in front. The last three are the ownership map from Partnership session 2, applied to this one ask.

LiveThe template, ready to paste4 min▶

Copy this into your ticket tool, a shared document, or a form. Keep it to these eight fields; every extra field is a reason not to use it.

★ The intake templateREQUESTER FILLS 1. Business question: what do you need to know, in one sentence? 2. Decision: what will you decide with the answer? (two or more possible actions) 3. Decision owner: who makes that decision and carries the outcome? 4. Deadline: when is the decision made? (the decision date, not the ask date) 5. What changes: what would you do differently on each possible answer? DATA TEAM ADDS 6. Knowledge tag: external market / internal evidence / both 7. Data's deliverable: what data hands over, and by when (from the service menu) not data's: ________________________ (owner: ______________) 8. Informed: who else is told when it lands

Field 5 is the one people skip and the one that matters most. If both answers lead to the same action, the analysis is not needed for this decision, and the honest move is to say so and offer what would change the action instead.

Self-studyThree ways the template gets gamed, and the fix for each3 min▶

"Decision: to understand the market." Understanding is not a decision. Ask what they will do once they understand it.

"Deadline: ASAP." ASAP has no date. Ask when the meeting is where the decision gets made.

"Decision owner: data." Then nobody in the business will act on it. The owner is the person who will be judged on the outcome; data informs the bet and the business places it.

Part 3 · covers the service menu

A one-page menu of what data offers

5 min live

A menu does two jobs. It tells marketing, brand and BD what they can ask for without negotiating, and how long each thing takes. And it says, on the same page, what is not on it and who owns that instead, which is the kindest form a boundary can take: written down before anybody asked.

LiveThe menu, ready to adapt3 min▶

Turnaround comes from the triage desk's modelled hours: at six request hours a working day, 2 hours or less is one working day, 3 to 6 hours is three working days, 8 to 10 hours is one to two weeks, and anything bigger is a project for the monthly plan. Your hours and days will differ; the bands are the idea.

Self-studyKeeping the menu honest2 min▶

A menu with turnaround times is a promise. Track how often each item lands on time for a quarter, and change the time on the menu rather than missing it quietly. Add an item when the same ask arrives three times in a month; remove one nobody has ordered in a quarter. And when capacity drops, as it did on the triage desk's break button, change the turnaround bands that month and tell everyone the same day.

Real world

The "not on the menu" list that ended an argument. A BD lead and a data lead had the same argument every quarter about who should do market research. Once the menu said, in a printed list both had agreed, that fieldwork sits with BD and an agency and that data supplies an evidence page in three days, the argument became a two-line request. Constructed example; the mechanism is writing the boundary down before the ask.

Build-along

The Japan ask, through the front door

★ 22 min · everyone works it through

No code. Kettle & Co's BD lead sends ask #11 from the triage desk on working day 8: "Can data do the market research on matcha drinkers in Japan?" Run it through the intake template, field by field, then match it to the menu and write the confirmation note. Then run one of your own asks the same way. Every Kettle & Co value is constructed; yours will differ.

Step 1 · Business question and decision. Ask the BD lead what she will decide. At Kettle & Co: whether to enter Japan next year. Write it as two possible actions.

★ Step 1 · fields 1 and 21 question: is there enough demand for matcha in Japan to enter next year? 2 decision: enter Japan next year, or not yours: ______________________________________________

Step 2 · Owner and deadline. The owner carries the outcome. The deadline is the decision date, here the agency brief, not "ASAP".

★ Step 2 · fields 3 and 43 owner: the BD lead 4 deadline: the agency brief, working day 15 yours: ________________ ________________

Step 3 · What changes. Make her say what she would do on each answer. If both answers lead to the same action, stop and say so.

★ Step 3 · field 5strong existing demand: brief the agency to look for distributors weak existing demand: park Japan for a year yours: ________________________________________________

Step 4 · Tag it. Mostly external: Japanese buyers, distributors and culture live outside our data. One internal thread: what our own orders and search data already say.

★ Step 4 · field 6tag: external, with one internal thread (Partnership session 2, row 11)

Step 5 · Match the menu. "Evidence page for market research", three working days. Lands day 8, due day 15: it fits. Write what is not data's in the same field, with its owner.

★ Step 5 · field 7data's: one page: existing Japan orders, repeat rate, search demand from Japan, by day 15 not data's: fieldwork and the go or no-go owner: the BD lead, with an agency 8 informed: marketing lead

Step 6 · Send the confirmation note. Two sentences, deliverable and date first, then the boundary with its reason. Partnership session 4 is all about this note.

★ Step 6 · the note"By day 15 you get one page on our existing Japan orders, their repeat rate and search demand from Japan, so you can brief the agency on what we already know. The fieldwork and the go or no-go stay with you, because you will own the Japan result."

Print the template and the menu together. Expand all, then print this page, or paste both into the place your requesters already work. A front door nobody can find is a wall.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Partnership session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · A requester fills in "Decision: to understand the market better". What should happen next?

Understanding is not a decision. The decision names the measure, the precision and the date; without it the analysis has no edge.

2 · Why does the service menu include a "not on the menu" list with owners?

A boundary written in advance is the kindest kind. The "what data can add" column keeps every split ask's evidence half alive.

3 · The Japan ask arrives as "can you do market research?". After intake, what does data deliver?

Three modelled hours instead of forty, and the decision stays with the person who will be judged on it.
