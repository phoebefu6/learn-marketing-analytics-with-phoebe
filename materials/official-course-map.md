## 1. The running case (constructed)

Kettle & Co: a fictional direct-to-consumer tea brand. People: a marketing lead, a brand manager,
a BD lead, a finance partner, an ops lead, and a data team of three analysts. Never Phoebe's own
employer, team or clients; never Cadence (the Marketing course's case). Every Kettle & Co number is
invented; pages that quote one say "constructed" and, where a learner would compute it, "your numbers
will differ".

## 2. The stance (the spine) and the alternative

- Market research and market penetration strategy are marketing's and BD's accountability. Data is
  consulted: it supplies internal evidence with a named deliverable and a date. Data does not take
  ownership of the marketing or BD job.
- The reason, stated on every page that touches it: whoever lives with the outcome owns the decision;
  an analyst who writes the strategy takes the decision away from its owner and ends up marking their
  own homework.
- Course phrasing (own words, never attributed as a quote): "Data informs the bet; the business
  places it."
- The alternative taught fairly (p2 Part 2): **market insights inside analytics** (model A). Fixes:
  one owner for the evidence including split rows, one method standard, one door. Costs: analysts
  drift into owning strategy; external research eats the capacity internal asks need. Works best when
  separately staffed and chartered as "evidence for the decision".
- Mentor lenses (paraphrased, never quoted on a page): decision-maker vs analyst split (decision
  intelligence writing, Cassie Kozyrkov); ownership as a domain boundary that needs a contract where
  domains meet (data mesh, Zhamak Dehghani); "consulted" must be a real job with deliverables
  (evidence-before-scaling stance, Mark Roberge); pushback opens with deliverable and date (Wes Kao).
  Pages name the lenses generically ("decision intelligence writers", "data mesh writers") unless a
  work below was read at source.

Model (modelled, declared on the widget): 3 analysts x 40 request hours = 120 hours a month,
20 working days, 6 hours a working day. Asks worked in landing order (day, then id). An ask cannot
start before the morning it lands; it is late if it finishes after its due day. Choices: Data owns
(hOwn), Data consulted - deliver X (hC), Redirect (0), Data takes all of it (hAll). "Starved" = an
ask whose correct answer is "Data owns" that is late. "Decided on anecdote" = a redirect on an ask
flagged as needing internal evidence. Match = choice equals the RACI answer.

## 6. Verified facts with tiers (raw notes and URLs: materials/sources-raw.md)

All fetched 2026-10-10. Platform facts: **re-verify the changelog before delivery.**

### Search (a2) - Google Search Central, read-at-source
- Three stages: crawling, indexing, serving. "Google Search works in three stages, and not all pages
  make it through each stage." Crawling is done by "automated programs called crawlers".
- "Google doesn't guarantee that it will crawl, index, or serve your page"; "Google doesn't accept
  payment to crawl a site more frequently, or rank it higher."
- SEO starter guide: changes "might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months"
  (matters for any before/after read of an SEO change).
- Data-team value (practice, not a source fact): Search Console queries joined to landing pages and
  orders; branded vs non-branded split; the lag means a before/after read needs weeks, not days.

### Email (a3) - Apple Mail Privacy Protection, read-at-source
- Announced in Apple's newsroom on 7 June 2021 (iOS 15 generation): stops senders "using invisible
  pixels", prevents senders "from knowing when they open an email", "masks their IP address".
- Mechanism (Apple legal privacy page): Mail "downloads remote content in the background by default",
  "regardless of whether you engage with the email". So pixel-based opens fire without a human open.
- Vendor (Litmus, June 2021, vendor tier): caching "makes the ESP think the email has been opened".
  Litmus's "around 75%" Apple open rate was a 2021 FORECAST, not a measurement: do not quote as data.
- Pages say: opens since MPP are inflated and unreliable as a success metric; use clicks, orders per
  send, and holdouts.

### Paid social (a3) - Meta Ads Help Center, read-at-source (browser)
- Attribution models: standard, incremental, custom. Click-through windows of 1 or 7 days, view-through
  1 day, engage-through 1 day (website/in-store, as listed 2026-10-10).
- "comparing across different attribution models will lead to inaccurate conclusions".
- The setting informs ad delivery as well as reporting ("inform ad delivery and determine how
  conversions can be credited").
- Meta "may use statistical modelling where conversion data may be missing or partial".
- Incremental attribution "uses machine learning models that predict whether a conversion is caused
  by an ad" (a model, not a holdout).
- The DEFAULT 7-day click / 1-day view is REPORTED only (Jon Loomer), not on Meta's page. Pages may
  say "commonly reported as the default"; never state it as Meta's wording.
- Conversion Lift (read-at-source): randomised test and control groups ("the people who don't see your
  ads"), "an intent-to-treat approach", lift = "the difference in conversions caused by the presence
  of your ads"; eligibility guide USD 5,000+ spend and 500 conversions; "no additional cost"; the lift
  metric "is estimated".
- Pages say: platform ROAS is the platform grading its own homework, under a window and a model the
  platform chose, partly modelled; incrementality comes from a holdout.

### Paid search (a3) - Google Ads Help, read-at-source
- Conversion modeling "uses observed conversions to predict unobserved conversions", included in
  reported totals "only when there's a high confidence"; needed "for cases where cookies and
  identifiers weren't available".
- Data-driven attribution is "the default attribution model for most conversion actions".
- Conversion Lift: control group of "people who don't see your ads"; users-based or geography-based
  splits; users-based "relies mostly on opt-in traffic"; not available for all accounts.
- REPORTED only: 1,000 conversions, USD 5,000 minimum, holdouts up to 50 percent. Do not quote.

### Brand vs activation (a1, a6) - mixed tiers
- 60:40 and the 996 IPA award-entry campaigns 1980-2010: REPORTED (Alex Murrell summary of The Long
  and the Short of It, IPA 2013). The IPA product page does not state the ratio.
- Effectiveness in Context (Binet and Field, 2018): headline 62:38 (read-at-source on an IPA-hosted
  opinion blog, Hoad, 13 Sep 2023); ratio varies "according to market category, business model,
  brand life stage and size" (read-at-source, Thinkbox landing page). Category splits (74:26 etc.):
  REPORTED only via System1, do not quote.
- B2B (Binet and Field, The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing, LinkedIn B2B Institute):
  read-at-source: "around 46%" brand, "around 54%" activation, "a rough estimate" from a small sample,
  IPA databank 1998-2018 B2B cases; a pull-quote in the same PDF says "a 50/50 split" (internal
  inconsistency; teach it).
- Critique: Byron Sharp, Mi3, 23 Aug 2022 (read-at-source report of a talk): rule "terrible, very
  misleading"; data are "award submissions"; activation undefined. Field's reply: REPORTED (FS Forum).
- Sibling consistency: learn-marketing-with-phoebe 06 teaches the same 60:40 with the award-entry
  caveat and the 46/54 vs 50/50 B2B disagreement. Consistent; no sibling fix needed.

### Mental and physical availability (a1) - Sharp
- Read-at-source via the Mi3 report: growth "largely depends on mental and physical availability";
  physical = "easy to buy"; mental = buyers "being able to notice you or even consider you"; they must
  overlap. The book (How Brands Grow, 2010) is REPORTED.

### RACI (p2) - PMI
- Read-at-source, PMI Blog, 29 July 2026 (not the PMBOK text): "Responsible, Accountable, Consulted,
  Informed"; "every task has exactly one Accountable person"; "There may be multiple Responsible
  parties"; Accountable = "the single person who owns the outcome and answers for it". PMBOK/Lexicon
  wording: REPORTED (login).

### Experiments and holdouts (a6) - Kohavi, Tang, Xu (CUP, 2020)
- Read-at-source: publisher description only. "Getting numbers is easy; getting numbers you can trust
  is hard." Authors' companies "each run more than 20,000 controlled experiments a year".
- The book's treatment of holdouts (ch. 23, long-term effects): REPORTED, not read. Pages cite the
  book generally for trustworthy experimentation, never for a specific holdout claim.

### Decision-maker lens (p2) - Kozyrkov
- HBR, 4 Dec 2018, "What Great Data Analysts Do - and Why Every Organization Needs Them": opening only
  read. Do NOT attribute the decision-maker split to her as a quote. Pages say "decision intelligence
  writers" generically.

Reading notes (what each table is built to teach; pages may say these):
- a2 search: the top-click query (matcha benefits, 5,100 clicks) sells 21 orders; "tea subscription uk"
  converts at 9.2 orders per 100 clicks; branded search is 610 of 871 orders. The insight worth a
  meeting: content that wins clicks is not content that sells; build for the buying queries.
- a2 social: followers +40 percent while social-referred orders went 121 to 128; the most-viewed post
  (cat video, 41,000 views) drove 2 orders; the launch post drove 63. Engagement content vs sales content.
- a3/a5 channels: paid search and paid social both show CAC 30, but paid social's 6-month repeat is 22
  vs 38 percent and it does not pay back within 6 months; affiliate has the cheapest paid CAC (15) and
  the worst repeat (14 percent): coupon sites (bench ask #8). Average CAC is not marginal CAC: the next
  6,000 on paid social buys 120 customers in the constructed response curve, marginal CAC 50.
- a4 cohorts: same lesson by month; paid social's curve flattens at 22 percent, organic search reaches 41.
- a6 email holdout: +0.007 orders per customer, 95% CI -0.014 to +0.028: no effect detected; the honest
  read says "we cannot tell it from zero at this size", not "email does nothing".
- a6 Meta: platform ROAS 4.1 vs incremental ROAS 1.56 from a lift test (38 percent incremental).
- a6 brand tracker: +3 points with a CI of -2.9 to +8.9: inside the noise of two n=500 waves.

Extra constructed figures (computed from the section 9 channel table, checked in Python 2026-10-10):
- a4 mix shift: blended 6-month repeat across all 2,670 Q3 new customers = 29.0 percent. If paid
  social's new customers double (800 -> 1,600) and every channel's own repeat rate is unchanged,
  blended repeat falls to 27.4 percent across 3,470. No channel got worse; the mix moved.
- a5 blended CAC 24.0 hides a channel spread from 8.0 (organic search, content cost) to 36.0 (creators).
- p3 service menu turnaround (constructed, from the bench hours at 6 request hours a working day):
